Rate nuclear per week from its highest observed output

In Balmorel’s base data, nuclear has a flat rating of 1 − GDPLANMAINT/8760 = 0.9425 all year. In the 2023 backcast that let France produce about 505 TWh against 319 TWh observed, because the year’s long outages are not in the base data. Nuclear runs whenever it is available, so its output is set by availability, not by dispatch. We therefore treat it as a Calibration Input, like the Observed Flow Limit (ADR 0010) and, later, the VRE Level (ADR 0012).

The Nuclear Availability of a country in one week of the Backcast Calendar is its highest hourly nuclear output that week, as reported by ENTSO-E, divided by its nuclear GKFX in the Backcast Year and clipped at

  1. nuclear-availability writes it as weekly GKRATE overrides to GKRATE_ENTSOE.inc. The scenario’s GKRATE.inc includes that file after the base ratings, which is the override-file pattern of ADR 0006. Every nuclear technology in a country gets the same rating, because ENTSO-E reports nuclear output per bidding zone, not per unit. The week’s highest output is a lower bound on what could run, for the same reason as the observed flow in ADR 0005: the fleet produced that much in at least one hour. A week is long enough to contain an outage and short enough to place it in the year. It is also Balmorel’s season, so the ratings fit GKRATE(AAA,GGG,SSS).

The weeks after a country’s last week with data are rated zero. ENTSO-E stops reporting a fleet that has closed instead of reporting zeros, as for Germany after 15 April 2023. A data feed that just stops looks the same, so the command warns loudly. Any other week without data keeps the base rating. A zero is written as eps, because Balmorel reads a literal 0 as full capacity.

Considered options

  • Balmorel’s own maintenance scheduling (PLANNED_MAINTENANCE): rejected. It places a generic amount of maintenance where it costs least, not when the outages of the Backcast Year happened. With the weekly ratings, it would also come on top of outages that the ratings already contain, so it is off in the backcast.

  • The week’s mean output: rejected. It fixes the week’s energy to the observed energy, and Balmorel could only reach it by running flat out in every hour. That removes the load following the fleet actually did within the week.

  • An annual energy limit (GMAXF): rejected. It says nothing about when the outages happened, so Balmorel would put its own outages in the cheapest hours.

  • ENTSO-E outage reports, as ADR 0014 uses for thermal capacity: not needed. Nuclear output is not a Validation Metric, so nothing argues against using observed output, and that output was already being downloaded for the validation.

Consequences

  • Each country’s annual nuclear output is fixed by a Calibration Input, so it is not evidence in Backcast Validation. How the fleet’s output varies within each week still is.

  • Because the rating comes from the week’s highest hour, Balmorel can match the observed output in every hour of the week. The exception is a rating clipped at 1: ENTSO-E reports Romania’s Cernavoda at up to 1,453 MW against 1,300 MW of GKFX, which is a reporting difference.

  • A GKFX that is too high is offset by a lower rating. A GKFX that is too low can’t be corrected by the rating, because ratings are clipped at

    1. Units that ran in the Backcast Year but are missing from base GKFX must therefore be added to the scenario’s GKFX. For 2023 these are Isar 2, Neckarwestheim 2, Emsland and Olkiluoto 3.

  • A country without ENTSO-E nuclear output keeps the base rating. In 2023 that is the UK.

  • The scenario needs GKRATE_DOL set to AAA_GGG_SSS, and PLANNED_MAINTENANCE off.
