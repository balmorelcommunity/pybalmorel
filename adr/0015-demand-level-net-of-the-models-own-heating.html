Take the Demand Level net of the model’s own electric heating, iterated over runs

Balmorel adds electric heating (ENDOGENOUS_ELECT2HEAT, E2H) and transmission losses on top of the exogenous demand DE. ENTSO-E’s load contains both. So the Demand Level has to leave out the heating Balmorel adds back, with the same scope, or every country’s load gap carries the difference into generation, prices and flows.

The first Demand Level, variant (a′), left out Eurostat’s electricity for heating, H: household space and water heating, plus electric boilers and heat pumps in district heating. Balmorel’s E2H covers more: industrial process heat, services heating, and individual users. In backcast_R2023 of 2026-11-04, solved with (a′), Balmorel’s load matched ENTSO-E’s at system level (2,694.7 against 2,699.6 TWh), but the country gaps summed to 175 TWh in absolute terms, nearly all of it E2H − H: IT +50.6 TWh (E2H − H +45.5), FR −25.5 (−24.7), NO −13.5 (−13.6). The system match itself hid offsetting errors (LU left out of the Balmorel side, DE 0.27% low). (a′) also counted transmission losses twice, about 20 TWh in that run, and the distribution losses Balmorel books on wind and solar output, about 7 TWh.

The Demand Level of canonical region k is now

X_k = (L_k − T_k − E_k − O_k)(1 − d_k)

with L_k ENTSO-E’s load, d_k the distribution loss, and T_k, E_k and O_k the previous backcast run’s transmission losses, electricity for heat, and distribution losses on anything but exogenous demand (on its electric heating, and the 1–2% of wind and solar output Balmorel books as distribution loss), summed over k’s Balmorel regions and scaled from the calendar’s 8736 hours to Balmorel’s 8760. Balmorel’s load as format compares it, EXO + E2H + DIST_LOSSES + TRANS_LOSSES, then equals L_k exactly, as long as E2H and the losses stay as they were.

They don’t, because E2H responds to prices: lower DE gives lower prices and more electric heating, roughly −0.3 TWh of E2H per TWh of DE in Europe (−0.06 in IT to −0.59 in ES, from two runs that differed in more than DE). The level is therefore a fixed-point iteration over runs, X_{n+1} = (L − T_n − E_n − O_n)(1 − d), which converges for any slope between −1 and 0, with the error shrinking by about that factor each run. demand-level --heat model is the default. Each run prints the previous run’s residual, X less that run’s exogenous load, summed over Europe and summed in absolute terms over countries. The user decides when to stop: nothing caps the number of runs, and about 10 TWh summed over countries is the guidance, since other errors remain. The first run of a new Backcast Year has no previous run, so it takes --heat eurostat, which is (a′).

Considered options

  • Keep (a′). It needs no previous run, and its system total was close. Rejected: the scope difference E2H − H lands in each country’s load, so a supply-side comparison per country inherits it (IT’s generation and imports are compared against 50 TWh of demand that ENTSO-E never saw).

  • Fix the scope of E2H in base data, so that Balmorel’s electric heating matches Eurostat’s scope and (a′) becomes exact. Rejected for now: it changes base data that other scenarios use (backcast corrections stay in override files, ADR 0006), Eurostat has no split of electric heating in services and industry to match, and transmission losses would still be in the gap.

  • An enforced stop rule or a relaxation factor (X_new = ωX + (1−ω)X_prev). Not built: with a slope near −0.3 plain iteration converges in two or three runs, and the user prefers to judge the residual. Relaxation can be added if a country oscillates.

Consequences

  • A country’s total load is a Calibration Input at every level, the system’s and each country’s, up to the last residual. The evidence on demand is E2H against H per country (E2H_minus_H_TWh in demand_level.csv), read knowing the scopes differ. IT’s E2H barely responds to prices, so its excess is structural (capacity or heat-demand allocation), and C moves it out of IT’s load rather than fixing it.

  • A backcast takes several runs, each a full-year hourly run (the check rejects a sampled run), and format must run on each before the next demand-level. The level depends on the previous run, so a change to any other input (capacities, fuel prices) moves the fixed point and needs another iteration or two.

  • The previous run must have solved with the scenario’s current DE_ENTSOE.inc. The command warns where its exogenous load is more than 1% off that file’s level, and warns if the INDUSTRY or INDIVUSERS wrappers that re-apply the level are missing.

  • A country without Eurostat balances (CH) gets a level under C, since only ENTSO-E load and the previous run are needed; its coverage check is skipped and noted.

  • The hourly shape stays ENTSO-E’s total load profile, which includes electric heating. Subtracting the previous run’s hourly heating from the profile as well is deferred.
