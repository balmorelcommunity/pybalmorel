Handover: demand level net of the model’s own electric heating

From the Balmorel backcast branch (2023 backcast, 2026-11-03). This follows handover-backcast-demand-level.md (variant (a′), implemented as demand-level). It changes which heating the level leaves out: the electricity the previous backcast run used for heat, instead of Eurostat’s figure. It also fixes an 8760/8736-hour mismatch, and adds an optional hourly refinement to demand-profile.

Status (2026-11-10): implemented as demand-level --heat model (the default), with --heat eurostat for the first run of a new year. See ADR 0015 and docs/preprocessing/preparing_backcast.md. Differences from this spec: CH is written (coverage unchecked), the residual is reported but no stop rule is enforced, the summary’s BaseTWh is InputTWh, and step 2 (the hourly refinement) is deferred. On 2026-11-11 the formula below was corrected to X_k = (L_k − T_k − E_k − O_k)(1 − d_k): Balmorel’s DIST_LOSSES also holds losses on its electric heating and on wind and solar output (DISLOSS_E_AG, 6.7 TWh in the compared countries), which this spec missed, so the residual disagreed with the load comparison by 7.3 TWh.

Earlier status: not built. This file was never committed, and it was restored from the 2026-11-03 session. The tables below come from backcast_R2023 of 2026-10-29, which was solved before the wrappers. backcast_R2023 of 2026-11-04 was solved with the wrappers and the current (a′) DE_ENTSOE.inc, so it can serve as iteration 0. In it, Balmorel’s load is 2,694.7 TWh against ENTSO-E’s 2,699.6. The per-country gaps add up to 175 TWh in absolute terms, nearly all of it E2H − H: IT +50.6 (E2H − H +45.5), FR −25.5 (−24.7), NO −13.5 (−13.6), and CH −11.6, which is on its base level. All runs before the thermal-outage fix of 2026-11-09 must be rerun anyway; that rerun can be iteration 0.

What happened

backcast_R2023 (solved 2026-10-29) was the first run with DE_ENTSOE.inc. Balmorel’s load came out 369 TWh below ENTSO-E’s (2,276 against 2,645 TWh). The worst countries were FR −77, DE −49, IT −43 and ES −39. Generation was correspondingly low.

Cause: the heating was subtracted twice. backcast/data/DE.inc applies DE_ENTSOE.inc while DE is read. bb4datainc.inc then runs the deadditions hook:

  • INDUSTRY_DE.inc subtracts DE1_IND from PII.

  • INDIVUSERS_DE.inc subtracts DE1_INDIVHEATING from RESE and OTHER.

Each clamps at 0. In the DE_ENTSOE regions that removed another 509 TWh (175 + 334) from a level that was already net of Eurostat heating (238 TWh). A minimal GAMS script (DE.inc plus the two hooks) reproduces the solved EXOGENOUS × 8760/8736 to 0.0 TWh in all 47 regions. So DE_ENTSOE.inc and DE_VAR_T_ENTSOE.inc themselves load correctly. The clamps had also zeroed RESE in IT, ES, PT, FIN and EE, and PII in DK1, DK2, LV and LT. Those zeros were carried into DE_ENTSOE.inc, because demand-level splits users by the post-addon DE shares in the input GDX.

Balmorel side (done). backcast/data/INDUSTRY_DE.inc and backcast/data/INDIVUSERS_DE.inc are new wrappers. Each includes base’s file of the same name, then DE_ENTSOE.inc again. The addons resolve ../data/<X>_DE.inc before base’s, so DE_ENTSOE.inc is now final whichever addon is on. Verified with the minimal script:

  • All 121 DE_ENTSOE cells are final.

  • The 18 other (region, user) cells are unchanged.

  • The DE_ENTSOE regions sum to 2,279.4 TWh after the hooks, against 1,771.9 before. IE was 36.2 then, from the all-island IE_SEM. With Ireland’s control area it is 29.1, and the total is 2,272.4.

Why (a′) is not enough

(a′) leaves out Eurostat’s electricity for heating, H: household space and water heating, plus electric boilers and heat pumps in district heating. Balmorel’s ENDOGENOUS_ELECT2HEAT (E2H) covers more than that: industrial process heat, services heating, and individual users in INDIVUSERS_DH / DH_TERTIARY. Under (a′) each country’s load gap is therefore (E2H − H) / (1 − d) plus transmission losses. That mixes a heating scope difference into every country’s load, and from there into generation, prices and flows. In backcast_R2023, E2H − H is IT +49.5, DE +32.3, ES +31.0 and FR +17.9 TWh (Europe +131). In the 2026-10-28 run IT was already +45.

Option C takes the heating out with the same scope Balmorel adds it back:

X_k = (L_k − T_k) × (1 − d_k) − E_k

  • L_k is ENTSO-E’s load of canonical region k over the Backcast Calendar, as now.

  • T_k and E_k are the previous run’s TRANS_LOSSES and ENDOGENOUS_ELECT2HEAT, summed over the 8736 hours and over k’s Balmorel regions.

  • d_k is DISLOSS_E, as now.

Balmorel’s compared load is (EXO + E2H) / (1 − d) + T. With EXO = X and unchanged E2H and T, it equals L exactly. E2H against H stays a Validation Metric, now outside the load.

E2H moves with the price, so this iterates

E2H is endogenous. Lower exogenous demand means lower prices, and so more electric heating. From the 2026-10-28 run to backcast_R2023:

  • EXO fell 476 TWh (2,243 → 1,767).

  • E2H rose 140 TWh (229 → 369).

  • That is about −0.3 TWh of E2H per TWh of EXO.

This is only a rough figure. The same commit also changed thermal availability, VRE levels and the must-run floor. Per country it varies a lot: FR −0.37, DE −0.19, IT −0.06, ES −0.59, SE −0.46, NO −0.34. IT’s E2H barely moves, so its excess is structural (capacity or heat-demand allocation) rather than a price effect.

Projections that hold E2H fixed are therefore misleading. Honouring (a′) with E2H fixed at 369 would overshoot by +167 TWh. Once E2H falls back towards its 2026-10-28 level (about 229 at an EXO of about 2,243), that overshoot mostly disappears. The plain fixed-point iteration X_{n+1} = (L − T)(1 − d) − E_n converges whenever −1 < slope ≤ 0. The error shrinks by |slope| each run. Starting point: the fixed point lies close to (a′), because E2H there (~218) is close to H (238). The first --heat model iteration should therefore take E2H from a run solved with the wrappers and the current (a′) DE_ENTSOE.inc, not from backcast_R2023. backcast_R2023’s E2H is inflated by its double-subtracted EXO. From a wrapper run, one or two C iterations should be enough. Mostly they move demand between countries: IT down by about 45, FR up by about 25.

Change

  1. Option --heat eurostat|model on demand-level, default model.

    • eurostat is today’s (a′): X_c = L_c(1 − d_c) − H_c.

    • model is option C.

    • Add --load-results <path> (default <scenario>/backcastoutput/balmorel_load.csv, which format writes). It is required for model. If the file is missing, the error should say: “run format on a solved backcast first, or use --heat eurostat for the first run of a new backcast year”.

  2. Read the previous run with a new function in demand.py, previous_run_heat(load_results, year) -> pd.DataFrame, indexed by Balmorel region with columns EXO, E2H and TLoss, all in MWh over the 8736 hours.

    • The file has one row per (Region, Season, Time, Category) with non-zero Value. Zero hours are dropped, so a region can have fewer than 8736 rows (108 in R2023).

    • Require exactly one Scenario and Year == year. Require 52 × 168 distinct (Season, Time) pairs across the whole file, which means an hourly full-year run. A full-year run with T_full has a seventh of that.

    • Categories: EXOGENOUS, ENDOGENOUS_ELECT2HEAT, TRANS_LOSSES. A missing category counts as 0. Ignore ENDO_INTRASTO and DIST_LOSSES.

  3. Consistency check, before DE_ENTSOE.inc is overwritten. Per region in the current DE_ENTSOE.inc, compare EXO with its DE × 8736/8760.

    • Where they differ by more than 1%, warn: “the previous run did not solve with this DE_ENTSOE.inc. The INDUSTRY_DE/INDIVUSERS_DE wrappers may be missing, or the file was regenerated since. Its E2H may be far from this level’s.”

    • Against backcast_R2023 this fires for every region (1,767 against 2,266 TWh). That is intended: R2023 is not a valid starting point.

    • Also warn if the scenario has no data/INDUSTRY_DE.inc or data/INDIVUSERS_DE.inc that includes DE_ENTSOE.inc, as is already done for DE.inc. Check only addons whose base file exists in base/data.

  4. Level per canonical region (model): X_k = NetLoad_k − TLoss_k × (1 − d_k) − E2H_k, where NetLoad_k = L_k(1 − d_k) as today.

    • E2H and T are per Balmorel region, so sum them over k’s regions (DE4-*), and use them directly for NO1-5, SE1-4 and DK1-2. No load share split of heat is needed, unlike H.

    • DE_LU: LU is its own Balmorel region with its own E2H. The LU share of the DE_LU load is split by FC_E as now. Then subtract each region’s own E2H and T.

    • Keep the coverage check, and the fallback to base for countries without ENTSO-E load or with coverage outside the bounds (AL, and UK and CH, which have no Eurostat data). Under model, H is no longer needed, so only the coverage check needs Eurostat. Skip that check where FC_E is missing, and say so in Note. CH, which has ENTSO-E load, is then written too.

    • Then split within k and over DEUSER as now.

  5. Hours: write DE = X × 8760 / 8736, for both --heat values. Balmorel’s EXOGENOUS summed over the 8736 modelled hours equals DE × 8736/8760 (verified to 0.0 TWh). L is summed over the same 8736 hours. Today’s DE_ENTSOE.inc is therefore 0.27% too low, 6.2 TWh for Europe.

  6. User shares. Once the input GDX is rebuilt with the wrappers in place (--reload-input), its DE for the DE_ENTSOE regions is the previous DE_ENTSOE.inc. The user split then stays at its fixed point, which is harmless. BaseTWh then means “previous level”, not base, though. Label the column InputTWh, or document this. The RESE/PII zeros inherited from the clamps are also harmless: OTHER and RESE get the same profile, and step 2 below makes the hourly sum right regardless of the split. Don’t fix them unless step 2 is skipped.

  7. Summary (backcastoutput/demand_level.csv).

    • Keep every current column, including the Eurostat H parts.

    • Add HeatSource (eurostat or model), PrevExoTWh, E2H_TWh, TransLossTWh, E2H_minus_H_TWh, and ResidualTWh = (L − T)(1 − d) − EXO − E2H (what the previous run missed, at final-consumption level).

    • Print the Europe total of ResidualTWh and the sum of its absolute values. These are the convergence measure: iterate until they are below about 10 TWh.

  8. Header of DE_ENTSOE.inc under model:

    * Electricity demand level for 2023: ENTSO-E load less transmission losses, net of
* distribution losses, less the electricity <run> used for heat
* (ENDOGENOUS_ELECT2HEAT, see CONTEXT.md) - generated, do not hand-edit

    <run> is the Scenario column of balmorel_load.csv.

Option, only if convergence is slow: --relaxation ω, with X_new = EXO_prev + ω(X_C − EXO_prev). A value of ω = 1 gives the plain update. Newton would be ω = 1/(1 + slope) ≈ 1.4. But IT’s slope is about 0, so ω > 1 overshoots there. Don’t add it unless needed.

Step 2: residual demand profile (demand-profile --heat model)

Today the Observed Demand Profile is ENTSO-E’s total load, including heating. Balmorel puts E2H’s own heating shape on top of it. In backcast_R2023 the monthly Balmorel/ENTSO-E ratio was 0.93 in January and 0.80 in summer. Part of that is the double-subtraction level error, but a winter-heavy E2H on a winter-heavy profile also bends the shape. Option: --heat none|model (default none, today’s behaviour), with the same --load-results.

For a canonical region k that has an ENTSO-E profile, at hour h:

R_k,h = L_k,h × (1 − T_k / L_k) × (1 − d_k) − Ē_k,h − P_k,h

  • Ē_k,h: the previous run’s E2H over k’s regions, averaged over hour h’s day. A day is the 24-term block T001–T024, T025–T048, … within each season; S01 T001 is 00:00 UTC on a Monday (calendar.py). The daily mean keeps the seasonal and weekly heating pattern but drops E2H’s intraday dispatch. That dispatch is a price response, and feeding it back would make the profile oscillate between runs.

  • P_k,h: PII’s exogenous hours from the input data: DE(PII) × DE_VAR_T(PII,h) / Σ_h DE_VAR_T(PII,h), summed over k’s regions, over the 8736 hours. demand-profile writes only OTHER/RESE, so this shape must be what OTHER + RESE add to PII.

  • Then Σ_h R_k,h = X_k − X_PII,k, consistent with step 1.

  • Clean as now: hours below PLAUSIBLE_LOW × median are interpolated. More than MAX_IMPLAUSIBLE_SHARE such hours means the residual is unreliable. Warn, and keep the total-load profile for that region.

  • All of k’s regions get R_k (DE4-* as now). Regions on the input-data fallback (UK, CY, LU, MT and TR; AL and BA when their ENTSO-E data fail) get no residual treatment.

  • Add to the report line: the source, the hours interpolated, and winter/summer ratio before → after.

Run it with the same balmorel_load.csv as demand-level. The level and shape then come from one run.

Tests

  • previous_run_heat: sparse rows sum per region; a missing category is 0; it rejects two scenarios, a wrong year, and a file with fewer than 8736 (S,T).

  • model, one region: X = (L − T)(1 − d) − E. With E2H and T unchanged, X/(1 − d) + E/(1 − d) + T = L.

  • DE4-*: E2H and T are summed over the four, and base’s ratios between them are kept.

  • NO1-5: each region uses its own E2H and T, and there is no load share split.

  • DE written = X × 8760/8736 under both --heat values.

  • The consistency warning fires when EXO differs from DE_ENTSOE × 8736/8760 by more than 1%, and stays silent within it.

  • The wrapper warning fires when data/INDIVUSERS_DE.inc is missing, and when it doesn’t include DE_ENTSOE.inc.

  • --heat eurostat is unchanged apart from the hours factor.

  • Residual profile: the daily mean of a toy E2H; PII subtracted; Σ R = X − X_PII; more than 2% non-positive hours falls back to total load.

Notes for the user

  • Run order:

    1. Solve the backcast with the wrappers and the current (a′) DE_ENTSOE.inc. This is iteration 0. It also confirms the wrapper fix in a solve.

    2. format.

    3. demand-level --heat model (and optionally demand-profile --heat model).

    4. Solve, and repeat steps 2-4 until the residual is below about 10 TWh.

  • What stays evidence: after convergence, a country’s total load (and, with step 2, its shape) matches ENTSO-E by construction. The evidence on demand is then E2H against Eurostat H per country. Read it knowing the scopes differ: E2H also covers industry and services. E2H_minus_H_TWh in the summary is that metric. The supply side (generation mix, prices, flows) no longer carries demand errors.

  • Transmission losses move with flows too. They are small (20 TWh for Europe) and converge with E2H.

Glossary (CONTEXT.md, Backcasting section)

Replace the Demand level entry once --heat model is the default:

**Demand level**:
The annual exogenous electricity demand (`DE`) of a region in the backcast year: its ENTSO-E load over the Backcast Calendar less the previous backcast run's transmission losses, net of Balmorel's distribution losses, less the electricity the previous run used for heat (`ENDOGENOUS_ELECT2HEAT`), which Balmorel adds back endogenously. Because that heating responds to prices, the level is iterated over runs until Balmorel's load matches ENTSO-E's. Taken from ENTSO-E's load rather than Eurostat's final consumption because the two differ in coverage (DE: ENTSO-E sees 7% less). Written as `DE` overrides to `DE_ENTSOE.inc`, which the backcast applies after the INDUSTRY and INDIVUSERS addons have adjusted `DE`. A calibration input: a country's total load is not evidence, its electric heating is (against Eurostat's electricity for heating).
_Avoid_: Final consumption (Eurostat's figure, with different coverage); Demand (ambiguous)

The Balmorel CONTEXT.md gets the same entry; keep the two in step.
